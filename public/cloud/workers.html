01

What a background worker is

A service that runs continuously, receives no web traffic, and processes jobs waiting in a queue.

02

Choosing the right service

Web service for requests, worker for queued jobs, cron job for schedules, workflow for long multi-step runs.

03

Worker architecture

Your app adds a job to the queue; a worker picks it up, does the work and saves the result to the database.

04

Task queues explained

A queue is a waiting line for jobs, so slow work never blocks the person using your app.

05

Healthcare job examples

Claim batches, EVV reconciliation, report generation, AI drafting and document reading.

06

Worker frameworks

Celery, Sidekiq, BullMQ, Asynq, Oban and apalis all run as standard background workers.

07

Managed queues

Postgres-backed or Redis-compatible queues, encrypted at rest, with no servers to manage.

08

Durable steps

Each finished step is saved, so a job that crashes resumes where it stopped instead of starting over.

09

Retries and failed jobs

Automatic retries with growing waits, and a holding area for jobs that keep failing.

10

Scheduling and priority

Run jobs now, later, on a schedule, or ahead of routine work when they are urgent.

11

Scaling workers

Set worker count by hand or scale automatically on queue length.

12

Protecting patient data in jobs

Encrypted job data, scrubbed logs and retention limits on finished jobs.

13

Monitoring workers

Queue length, failure rate, run history and alerts in one view.

14

Worker costs

Pay for the compute time your workers actually use.

15

Your first worker

Copy-ready starter code for Node.js and Python.

16

Moving existing workers

Step-by-step migration from another host with no lost jobs.

17

Common questions

Workers can call any service over the private network and never need a public address.
